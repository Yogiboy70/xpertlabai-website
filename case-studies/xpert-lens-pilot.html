The question

Would people trust what an AI interviewer concluded about them?

An AI interview is only useful if its conclusions ring true — to the employer reading the report and to the person it describes. Before taking Xpert Lens to market, we wanted the hardest possible test: show participants exactly what the AI concluded about them, and ask them to rate it.

Fifty participants completed the full Xpert Lens flow: a live, spoken conversation with an AI interviewer, a short timed and proctored assessment, and then their own report — with every finding linked to the words they had actually said.

The results

45 five-star ratings. 4 four-star. 1 three-star. None lower.

★★★★★  45 participants90%
★★★★☆  4 participants8%
★★★☆☆  1 participant2%

Nine in ten participants gave the findings the top rating, and 49 of 50 rated them four stars or higher — an average of 4.88 out of 5.

We believe the reason is the design principle behind the report: evidence, not assertion. Xpert Lens doesn't simply tell someone they communicate clearly or think strategically — it shows them where in the conversation they did.

What it means for employers

A first round you can rely on — and candidates who feel heard.

For hiring teams, the pilot is a signal that Xpert Lens can run consistent, structured first-round interviews at scale, with conclusions candidates themselves recognise as fair. Final-round interviews start from evidence, not from a blank page.

Xpert Lens works on its own or inside Xpert ATS, where every shortlisted candidate can be sent a live interview link with one click.
